## The LOWs

**LOW-1, the read-fault branch.** `output_spent` now answers a FAULTED record
read with the fault (`LookupServiceError::StorageError`, "not now"). The engine
treats a notification fault as a fault before the transaction landed (#657):
the coin stays held and unapplied, and the replay tells the hook again. Proven
through the real engine by `fix936f_l1`: with one faulted read, the refund does
not land and the JOIN's coin stays; the replay records the pot and spends it.
On `5eb071da` the refund landed (`applied_topics: ["tm_pot"]`, the coin
deleted, no record, the replay a dupe). The lens's premise for the old branch
was that an admit write resets a verified latch. It does not hold on either
backend: D1's `store_beef` probes first and writes nothing over a verified row,
and the memory store does the same. Its cost: a transient read fault on any
pot's spend now replays the spender where it used to write the pointer with no
verdict. A pot row that deterministically fails to read would hold its spender
at the door (three retries, then a dead letter). No such row is known.

**LOW-2, a spender that does not carry the pot.** The pot's bytes now come from
the source the spender carries, or else from the ENGINE's own stored copy
(`PotStorage::held_transaction_beef`). The trait default is none. The D1
backend runs the existing `admission_abort::STORED_BEEF_SQL` over
`transactions`; that row still stands at the hook, because the engine deletes
the stale coin after its notifications (#559). Both sources are hash-bound: the
atomic BEEF's subject id must equal the outpoint's txid (`held_output_bytes`).
`fix936f_l2`: recorded from the copy, while a copy naming another transaction
records nothing (RED on the tip: nothing recorded). The lens's other half (a
proven spend reachable on LOW) is not measured. The fold covers it whether or
not it is reachable.

**LOW-3, a record without its bytes.** At the spend of a RECORDED pot, the
store is probed for the pot's bytes (`PotStorage::has_beef`; D1 runs
`POT_BEEF_PROBE_SQL`, which reads no bytes). If none are held, they are written
from the same two sources (`complete_held_output_bytes`). A row that is present,
verified or not, is never touched. A probe or write fault is the hook's fault.
This closes the heal's own window and also an ADMISSION cut between
`store_record` and `store_beef`, which CLAUDE.md had stated as a residual.
`fix936f_l3` covers both shapes (RED on the tip: no bytes). Its cost: one
primary-key read for each spend of a `tm_pot` or `tm_lowfund` outpoint.
