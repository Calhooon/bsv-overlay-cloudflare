## 2. Question 1, MONEY: the hold, the note and the order

### 2.1 What the hold is, and when it is written

The live hold is a D1 predicate: `stored::LIVE_HOLD` (stored.rs:236), read by `live_hold` (runtime.rs:544-556) from
the app layer's own `OVERLAY_DB` binding; its twin at the overlay is `LIVE_HOLD_SQL` (tracker_events.rs:61, used by
`note_held`): a `transactions` row, or `pot_records.spendingTxid` naming the subject. No read replication is in use
(no D1 session API in either crate), so a row the overlay committed is the row the app layer reads.

The pot note is a per-isolate in-memory trigger (`pot_changes::note`, pot_changes.rs:27-36), made AFTER the D1
statement it reports: `mark_spent` executes its UPDATE (d1_discovery.rs:2757-2761, awaited) and notes at :2771;
`store_record` notes at :2710 after its writes; the spender-facts CAS notes at :2644. The note is never what the hold
reads. So a word that reaches the app layer "before the pot note it belongs to" cannot be un-held by that: by the
time any note exists, the row it reports is in D1. The latch (`latch_network_seen`, ops.rs:1103-1152) queues the
`seen` frame (:1113-1117) and then reads the pots the txid SPENDS with `NETWORK_SEEN_SPENT_POT_ROWS_SQL` (:1171-1172,
`pot_records WHERE spendingTxid = lower(?)`), the same row the hold reads: a latch that notes a spent pot is a latch
whose word is held, in either order of the flush.

### 2.2 Which order is right for a settle, and does it hold for the JOIN and the hop sweep

A settle admits no output and never has a `transactions` row (#757). Its `submit` frame (`built`) is queued by
`note_submit` after the engine write (routes.rs:2512, after the submit at :2019) and its `seen` by the latch; the pot
note comes from `mark_spent`. `/internal/pot-changed` answers first and runs the owed recompute in `wait_until`
(routes.rs:3015-3033, `owed_recompute_and_push_coalesced` :6428); the recompute reads the spender's word through
`views` (routes.rs:4781) into `coin_landing` (owed.rs:2740-2756): `built` is `Withheld`, `seen` with final bytes
`Awaiting` (the row tells the outcome), a checked `mined` `Landed`. The words first is the right order: the word is
the evidence, the note a trigger.

The JOIN admits its outputs (a `transactions` row); its hop rows' spender word is read by `apply_word_arm` over the
hops view (owed.rs:2787-2800); its pot note (`store_record`) and hop notes (`hop_changes`, from the markers) ride the
same flush. The hop sweep's hold is the hop row's `spendingTxid` (a hop row is a `pot_records` row; `mark_spent`, or
`discover_missing_spends`' courier-found pointer); its `seen` comes from the latch (the Collect press is a gated
submit) and the latch's spent read covers hop rows; its `proof` from the chaser (proof_fetcher.rs:1205) beside the
confirm's note. One rule for all three subjects and all three flush points. The one arm outside the rule is the Skip
arm (L1): the notes parked for the cron, the frames left in memory, the notes first again on that path.

### 2.3 The case the order does not reach (M1)

The dual push task is spawned at routes.rs:1550 and the pending watch at :1720 and :1823, all in `wait_until`,
BEFORE the engine write at :1980 and :2019; a `wait_until` future runs interleaved with the handler from the moment it
is spawned. The watch's first look is 2 s in (`PENDING_WATCH_SLEEPS_MS`, admit_fast.rs:2570); the dual push's
corroboration answers in about a second (#660's own record: the latch 1.3 s after the hook). FIX-838 measured the
door's step 4 (the engine's write and its predecessor reads) at p50 2,566 ms, p95 12,903 ms, max 23,116 ms on the
fleet (`docs/audit/FIX-838-door-latency-2026-10-22.md`, finding 2). Where the latch wins that race:

1. `latch_network_seen` inserts the latch and queues `seen`; its spent read (ops.rs:1141) finds no row (the mark is
   in flight) and notes nothing; the job ships its own flush (`flush_inline_within`, admit_fast.rs:2625;
   `flush_inline`, routes.rs:1568), the words first.
2. At the app layer `live_hold` answers false, `event_for_subject` returns `Ok(None)` (runtime.rs:508-520), `ingest`
   answers `{"ok":true}` (:701-717); the overlay counts the frame `shipped`. The `seen` is gone.
3. The engine write lands, `mark_spent` notes the pot, `note_submit` queues `built`; the request's flush ships `built`
   then the note; the recompute reads `built`: `Withheld`.
4. Nothing recovers the `seen` before the block: `hook_may_reask` is tick-only (runtime.rs:933) and `execute_reask`
   returns a proof, never a hint (tracker_host/mod.rs:172-220); #757's own words: "never on seen". The MINED callback
   (`/arc-ingest`, the `proof` frame, held by then) lands the row at the block.

The commit's order is right and does not reach this: the word's flush precedes the hold's write by seconds. #757
states the limit ("a `seen` that reaches the app layer before the mark is dropped"); this lens shows it is the door's
own load path, not a replay corner. Not money: the claimable arm rests on the index's proof (#736) and no press is
offered on `built`. It is #660's symptom for the block interval (ten minutes mean, the Poisson tail an hour), filed
HIGH when #660 was filed. Unmeasured, and unmeasurable today (L2).

**Fold M1, the mark re-notes the latch that beat it.** At the submit route after the engine write, beside

row, ship: `seen`. RED on b29b684c at the last assertion (`built`): nothing re-notes.

## 3. Question 2: a flush that fails after the words and before the notes

The wall: a word frame at or past the deadline is counted `walled` and `failed` (tracker_events.rs:125-128), which
re-enqueues it once with `retry` (:90-97) for the next flush; a hop or pot chunk at the deadline is deferred
(change_flush.rs:120-122) and noted back (`settle`, :211-250) in memory. Neither is doubled. The runtime's cancel
(the 30 s `wait_until` window, pot_changes.rs:228): every word POST already answered is written at the app layer,
which writes before it answers (`event_held` ends in `stored::write`, runtime.rs:698; `ingest` answers after it,
:716); the notes drained for that task are lost with it, the stated hole (change_flush.rs module doc, "THE NOTE-BACK
IS NOT DURABLE"; pot_changes.rs:200-208): the receiver's cadence, never money, and the per-tx MINED callback's own
flush notes the pot at the block. A word POST whose answer is lost is `failed` and re-POSTed once: the second delivery
seeds the state from the stored row (`seed_hint`, stored.rs:114-146), re-steps the same hint and the UPSERT
(stored.rs:13) keeps the word and moves `updated_at` forward: idempotent in the word, counted `shipped` twice (N3).
The old order lost the WORDS in the same cancel (their own 20 s deadline in `flush` and `flush_inline` stacked past
the window, uncounted); the new order loses the notes, which have the read cadence and the block. The better side.

## 4. Question 3: the cost of the words first

Per frame at the app layer (`event_held`, runtime.rs:530-704): `live_hold` (one statement), `stored::read` (one),
`held_proof` (up to two, every kind but `proof`, :422-434), the write (one) and a counter take; a `callback` frame
adds a live Arcade `GET /tx/{txid}` (:617-631), a pushed proof a CHAINTRACKS headers call (:646): three to five
awaited D1 statements and up to two subrequests. The flush ships frames one POST each, awaited
(`ship_until`, tracker_events.rs:119-141), up to the EVENTS cap of 128 (:31), and stops STARTING a POST at the deadline
(:125). So the cost is bounded by the wall (`FLUSH_WALL_BUDGET_MS` 20 s, change_flush.rs:97) and the cap, never by the
frames' own cost. What the bound costs: at FIX-838's measured queue (about 175 ms per awaited statement, 5,434 ms over
31 on the fleet's D1) a frame is 0.5 to 1 s, some 20 to 40 frames fit the wall, the rest retry once, and EVERY hop and
pot note of that flush is deferred (`ship_chunks` starts none at the deadline) to the isolate's next flush: for a cron
isolate the next tick, for a request isolate the next request routed there, unbounded in time.

Where a flood arises: not the callback route (`/arc-ingest` carries one txid per POST, `classify_arc_ingest_body`,
routes.rs:3092-3127), but the tip pass (tip_pass.rs:361, 661, 820), the cron (tick.rs:115) and the queue batch
(lib.rs:2139): a block confirming N spends queues N `proof` frames (`verify_bump_detailed`, proof_fetcher.rs:1205)
beside N pot notes, and those are exactly the flushes whose notes need those words. Before the commit the same flood
starved the words. Both orders converge at the block through the per-tx MINED callback's own flush. The lane did not
measure this, and neither did this lens (no live or route-tier run): the per-statement figure is FIX-838's.

**Fold L3, a reserved slice.** In the one order (F5's `ship_sets`): the words under `min(deadline, start +
WORDS_SHARE_MS)` with `WORDS_SHARE_MS = 10_000`, then the hop and pot notes under the deadline, then the words left
under the deadline (a walled frame keeps its once retry). Pin: the fake clock of `the_worst_case_flush_is_bounded_in_posts_bytes_and_wall`
(change_flush.rs:687), 40 word frames at 1 s each and 16 pot chunks: with the slice the 16 chunks ship and 30 frames
retry; without it (RED on b29b684c's order) every chunk defers.
