## 1. Durability of the answer: holds

The order on every path of one frame (`ingest`, `runtime.rs:764`, through `ingest_frame`, `runtime.rs:744`):

- `ingest_frame` awaits `apply` (the event) at `runtime.rs:756` with `?`; only after it returns `Ok` is the judge handed
  the txid (`runtime.rs:758`) and the answer built (`runtime.rs:761`). The judge closure is synchronous (`J:
  FnOnce(String)`); the route's closure only calls `ctx.wait_until` (`runtime.rs:778`), so nothing of the judge is
  awaited before the answer.
- `event_held` ends at `land_word(&D1Sql::of(env)?, &row).await.map_err(..)?` (`runtime.rs:705`), which is
  `write_word` (`owed_judge/writers.rs:106`): the same UPSERT in its own batch through `D1Sql::run_batch`
  (`routes.rs:3142`, `counted_batch`, an error is `Err`). So the 200 follows a committed batch.
- A D1 fault at the UPSERT (or at `stored::read`, `held_proof`, `live_hold`, `D1Sql::of`): `Err` up through
  `ingest_frame` and `ingest`; the route answers an error status; the overlay's `post_body_answer`
  (`change_flush.rs:441`) is `Err` on a non-2xx, so the frame is refused and backed off. No 200.
- A frame for a subject with no live hold (and not `submit`): `event` answers `None` after `live_hold`; the answer is
  `{"held":false}`, the overlay's `answered_held` reads false and refuses it (pre-existing, #660; unchanged).
- A duplicate frame: the UPSERT is idempotent; the answer is held and the frame unparks; the detached judge runs again
  over whatever marks are pending (no harm, cost only).
- A frame past the overlay's 10 s wall (`change_park.rs:524-526`): the race drops the future; the app layer may still
  land the word and answer into nothing; the overlay refuses and re-sends; the re-send is an idempotent UPSERT. The
  wrong direction (a 200 read as held for a word that did not land) needs a 2xx without a write: not reachable (below).
- The one held answer without a write: `kind == "fallback"` with a stored row returns `Ok(old)` at `runtime.rs:585`,
  before any write. It was so on the base too (it returned before the judge), and the overlay never sends `fallback`
  (its producers note `seen`, `submit`, `proof`, `callback`, `accepted`). Since this PR such a frame also detaches a
  judge it did not run on the base: cost only, unreachable from the producer. NOTE-1.

Edge, pre-existing and unchanged: `answered_held` reads a body that does not parse as held (`tracker_events.rs:150`,
`unwrap_or(true)`), and `post_body_answer` turns a 2xx whose body read fails into `""`. A 2xx from this route is only
`ingest_answer`, built after the write, so this does not reach a lost word here.

## 2. The detached judge under `ctx.wait_until`: holds

- The bound: `worker_deadline(OWED_DRAIN_WALL_MS, OWED_DRAIN_RESERVE_MS)` (`owed_belt.rs:31-32`, 20 s less 3 s) built
  INSIDE the `wait_until` future (`runtime.rs:779-782`), so the 17 s run from the judge's first poll, after the answer;
  the tip read is inside `judge_word` (`runtime.rs:727`), so inside the wall, as the record door's
  (`record_post/detached.rs:40-46`). 17 s is under the platform's 30 s `wait_until` grace.
- Per subject atomic: `drain_changed` (`writers.rs:55`) judges each marked subject by its own `judge_subjects` call;
  `judge_with_write` reads the marks (`owed_judge.rs:447`) and hydrates (`owed_judge.rs:461`), then lands the per-game
  delete, the rows, the word references, the state and the generation-bounded acknowledgement in ONE batch
  (`owed_judge.rs:555`). A cut before that batch leaves nothing of the subject and its marks; a cut after it leaves the
  subject whole. No half row.
- The marks survive a cut: they are written by the `tx_words` triggers inside the UPSERT, before the answer, and are
  only deleted by the acknowledgement in the commit batch. A judge cut, faulted (`Some(Err)`, `runtime.rs:785`) or walled
  (`None`, `runtime.rs:788`) leaves them for the belt actor's alarm drain (`owed_belt::drain_env`, 64 subjects a turn
  every `OWED_BELT_TICK_MS` 120 s) and the scheduled pass.
- `claimable` from a half-judged state: no. A subject's rows are served only as a committed batch wrote them; until
  then `/owed` serves the previous judgement, with a DOWNGRADE-class pending mark withholding its presses (the D2-L1
  read rule). A settle's `mined` word is an upgrade: the Collect press appears only when a judge commits.
- What a cut loses: the relay push after the commit (`owed_judge.rs:571`) when the cut falls between the batch and the
  push; the rows are right and the client sees them on its next read. Same as the record door. NOTE-2.
- Unverified: whether a service-binding callee's `wait_until` outlives the CALLER's invocation (the overlay's
  `/admin/complete-proofs` or its cron). If the platform ends it with the caller, the judge is cut and the marks wait
  for the drain: the same safe outcome, a later `claimable`. NOTE-3.

## 3. Load: the bound is the wall, not a budget (LOW-1)

The detached judge keeps the 16-subject cap per invocation (`CHANGED_SQL` `LIMIT ?3`, `OWED_JUDGE_SUBJECTS_PER_INVOCATION`
16, `writers.rs:62-66`), but there is no shared per-pass budget: each POST is its own invocation, the producer starts up
to `DRAIN_MAX_ROWS` 128 at once (`change_park.rs:525`), so one drain pass starts up to 128 detached judges, each up to 16
subjects: 2,048 subject judgements a pass, the same ceiling as the base (whose 121 inline judges ran at once too).

Worst case, by the tier's measured costs (no new measurement; local D1): a single-subject judge costs about 39
statements (`owed-belt-tick`, 313 statements for 8 finds, each find one subject's commit) and 112 rows read, up to about
2,100 rows on a 500-row identity (the belt paragraph of CLAUDE.md). Per invocation: 16 x 39 = about 624 statements,
under D1's 1,000 queries per invocation; up to 33,600 rows read on whales. Per pass: 128 x 624 = about 80,000
statements and up to about 4.3 M rows read (prod's daily budget is 20 M). CHAINTRACKS: a judge is sequential and bounded
by its wall, so at most 17 s / round trip calls a judge: at the storm's 740 ms median about 23, so about 2,900 a pass
(L52 measured 1,870 for 121 handlers over 100 s). These are per pass; the overlay runs a pass on its cron and on each
`/admin/complete-proofs`.

What changes against the base: the base's frames were refused and re-sent up to `CHANGE_PARK_MAX_ATTEMPTS` 8 times,
each re-send running the judge again (1,743 exhausted rows on beta); after the fix a frame is answered once and
unparks, so the judges per frame fall from up to 8 to 1. The fleet load per word is lower; the burst per pass is not.

The finding: under the storm L52 measured, the first commit of an inline judge came 33 to 49 s into the judge (22 of 121;
99 committed none in about 97 s). The detached judge's wall is 17 s. If the burst after the fix is like the burst before
it, the 128 detached judges spend their whole wall in D1 and CHAINTRACKS reads and commit nothing, and every subject is
judged by the alarm drain, which is itself starved by the same burst (L52: `owed drain reached its wall` with 16
subjects chosen). Load with no yield. Fold (LOW): bound the detached judges per isolate (one in flight, a second frame's
judge skipped and its marks left to the drain), or measure at the land that a pass's detached judges commit; either is a
small change to the closure at `runtime.rs:776-791`. Not a correctness matter: the marks are durable either way.

`/health/invariants.costBar` sees the D1 statements and rows, late: the detached statements are noted to the isolate's
unscoped tally (the request's scoped ledger closed at the answer) and written with a later request's cost window take
(64 requests or 60 s), lost with an isolate's unwritten tail. It does not see CHAINTRACKS subrequests at all. The
route's own `Server-Timing: d1` no longer includes the judge. LOW-4: state it in the lane's doc; no tier ceiling names
this route today.

  the cure (DESIGN-D2-ACTOR section 3).
